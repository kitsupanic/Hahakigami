# Scan disks in parallel on one shared budget

With a scan per disk (ADR 0003), the only thing left that forced one scan at a time was a gate: any running scan refused every start. Each scan picked its own workers (up to 16) and its own open-file budget (`RLIMIT_NOFILE` less 64, clamped 1..512) and knew nothing of the others, so three scans on three disks could oversubscribe the CPU and, with a low descriptor limit, run the process out of file descriptors. Disks are independent, so the gate is now per slot: a disk (or the folder slot) that is already scanning cannot start again, and every other slot can start at any time. The scans share one process-wide budget instead of each assuming it has the machine.

## The budget

- Total workers are `min(CPU threads, 16)`, the most one scan's settings allow. Total retained descriptors are `disk_scan::default_descriptor_limit()`, the value a lone scan used to get. A scan takes a share when it starts (`budget::Budget::grant`) and gives it back when its `Grant` drops. Scans already running keep what they hold. Nothing is rebalanced while they run: a scan's worker count and descriptor limit are fixed at its start, as before.
- **Workers never refuse a start.** Alone, a scan gets what it asks for, so a single scan keeps its worker count. Beside other scans it gets at most half of what is still free (rounded up), but at least one. When the earlier scans hold every worker, the next one still starts with one and the process runs a thread or two over its total. This keeps the main case working on a small Mac: a volume scan asks for four workers on a four-thread machine, takes all four, and a second disk still starts with one. It also means a scan stuck on a dead mount, which holds its workers until its thread ends, cannot lock later scans out. The cost is that the second and later scans are slower than they could be; no worker is taken back from a running scan.
- **Descriptors are a hard total**, because the process limit is. A scan asks for half the total: it never needs more (the retained-descriptor peak was 21 to 57 of 512 on trees of 40 thousand to 1.4 million entries), and the rest stays for scans that start later. Alone it gets that half. Beside others it gets half of what is free, never less than 8 (`MIN_DESCRIPTORS`, enough for its root and a few parents). With the usual 512 the shares run 256, 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 8, so seven scans are admitted and the eighth is refused with a message, before anything is replaced. Past its limit the scanner falls back to path walks, as it always did, so a small share costs speed, not correctness.
- The process limit starts at 256 for an app launched from Finder, which would make the total 192 and the shares 96, 48, 24, 12 (below the measured peak after the second scan). So `budget::raise_descriptor_limit()` runs at the start of `run()`, before `AppState` reads the limit. It raises this process's own soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` toward its hard limit, capped at 10240 because macOS rejects `RLIM_INFINITY` and anything above `OPEN_MAX` for the soft limit. If the system refuses, the old limit stays. It touches only this process: no launchctl and no system setting. With a soft limit of 576 or more the total is the scanner's cap of 512.
- A scan asks for the larger of its worker count and its cached-validation worker count (incremental rescans can widen to the latter). The share is carried by `ScanOptions::descriptor_limit` (new, `None` keeps the old default) and the capped worker counts on the session. Every scanner call of the scan uses them, including the speculative rescan and clone validation.
- The `Grant` is owned by the scan thread and dropped just before the scan reads as ended, so it returns on completion, cancel, failure and a panic. A discarded scan that is still winding down holds its share until its thread ends.
- A panic in a scan thread is caught at the thread's top (`catch_unwind`, as calibration does). The session ends as Failed (a scan that had already finished keeps its results and gets a cache warning), `finishing` is cleared, and a final event is sent, so a panic cannot leave a slot reading as running forever with every "no scan at all" gate closed. The rescan cache lock is poison tolerant, so a scan that panicked while holding it does not make every other scan panic.

## What still cannot overlap

- **Calibration, first-run setup and restart, saved-scan open, import, export and compare** need no scan at all, a winding-down one included (`LiveSlots::any_running`). Calibration times a sample walk at full width, restart ends the process, and the saved-scan operations replace or read the one open archive. Calibration also refuses new scans while it runs.
- **The activity gate** keeps one exclusive operation at a time (library, cleanup, queue edits, Quick Look, reveal, restart, calibration). Scan starts hold it shared, so several can begin together, and an exclusive operation and a start still refuse each other. This is what keeps a "no scan running" check true until its operation is done.
- A cleanup on one disk waits for no other disk, and it invalidates only its own session.
- A winding-down scan no longer blocks starts: it only keeps its budget share and the gates above.

## Consequences

- The rescan cache stays one shared mutex-guarded store. Publication (`save`) holds the lock for the whole call and so does the short record check before a baseline loads: that is what keeps another scan's sweep of leftover files from removing an archive that is written but not yet recorded. Scans that finish together publish one after the other with the limits (8 entries, 512 MB) applied inside the lock, so there are no torn writes and the cap holds.
- Decoding a cached baseline does not hold the lock. `begin_load` confirms the record under it, and the archive decodes outside it (`Pending::decode`). A published archive never changes, so another scan's pruning can at worst remove it, and then the decode fails and the scan falls back to a full one. One disk's decode therefore no longer delays another disk's publication.
- A scan that starts while another publishes still waits for the lock, which `save` holds for seconds on a large scan, so its first progress can be late. Moving the encode out of the lock would need the sweep to learn about in-flight writes, so it stays.
- At most seven scans start at once with the usual 512 descriptors; an eighth is refused until one finishes. Workers may exceed their total by the one-worker minimum of each scan started after they ran out.
- Starts can overlap in the frontend, so each start registers its slot on its own channel and only the newest takes the view; `busy` counts starts in flight.
- The cleanup queue matches each entry to the scan of its own root or a disk scan that covers it (ADR 0003), so a scan still running on one disk makes only the entries that depend on it read "needs scan".
